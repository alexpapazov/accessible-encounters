# The Plan 75 consultation

> **For reviewers.** This is the complete text of one case, in reading
> order. You do not need to use the app. Questions worth answering as you read:
>
> 1. **Does any choice read as obviously correct?** Every option should be
>    defensible from some stakeholder's position. Flag any that isn't.
> 2. **Are the patients people, or teaching devices?** Flag anything that
>    reduces a character to their diagnosis, identity, or a lesson.
> 3. **Is the clinical content accurate and current?**
> 4. **Does the feedback explain rather than grade?** Flag anything that
>    reads as "right answer / wrong answer."
> 5. **Whose experience is missing or misrepresented?**
>
> Send notes back in any form. Review status only changes to
> `expert-reviewed` after a named reviewer with relevant lived or
> professional expertise has actually read the case.

- **Case id:** `the-plan-75-consultation` (v1)
- **Setting:** Outpatient clinic, three appointments over three weeks
- **Difficulty:** advanced · **Modes:** deliberative · **Scoring:** standard
- **Review status:** draft

## Characters

### You (clinician)

### Toshio Arai (patient)

> Toshio Arai: seventy-nine, widowed, worked at a municipal depot for forty years. He lives alone in a housing block and lost his cleaning shift when the contract changed hands. His rent goes up in the spring. His blood pressure is well controlled.

### Keiko Arai (family-member)

> Keiko Arai: Toshio's younger sister. She lives in another prefecture. They have not spoken in six years.

### Dr. Sekine (supervisor)

## Learning objectives

- Tell whether a request is a settled preference or the shape an unmet need has taken
- Recognise that a negative depression screen does not answer the question
- Ask what the eligibility form does not ask
- Respect a decision you would not make without processing it unexamined

## Node: What he came to ask `the-question` *(start)*

*day 1 · morning · present — Toshio Arai, You · Toshio Arai: neutral*

**Situation:**

Toshio Arai comes in for a blood pressure check. His readings are fine. At the end he takes a Plan 75 information packet out of his bag and puts it on the desk. He asks whether enrolling would be reasonable for someone like him. He is seventy-nine, he is eligible, and nothing about the way he asks suggests he is confused. The clinic requires you to give the program information on request and to complete eligibility documentation without delay.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio has read the packet twice. He wants an answer from someone whose job it is to know, and he expects to be told he is being silly. He has decided in advance not to argue if that happens.

### Choice 1 `tq-neutral`

**Give him the program information and answer what he asks about it.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity +1, Trust and therapeutic relationship -1, Professional integrity -1*

*Next: → `the-screen`*

**Immediate:** You go through the packet with him. He asks about the payment and about how long the wait is. He does not ask anything else.

**Institutional response:** This is what the clinic asks for, delivered in one appointment.

**Ethical interpretation:** He got accurate information and was treated as capable of using it. Nothing was asked about why he is asking.

**Delayed (at node `what-the-form-does-not-ask`):** Toshio kept the packet in his bag and brought it to every appointment after that.

### Choice 2 `tq-what-changed`

**Ask him what has changed since the last time you saw him.**

*Effects: Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Agency and dignity +1, Professional integrity +1, Operational efficiency -2*

*Next: → `the-screen`*

**Immediate:** The cleaning contract went to another company in March. The rent goes up in the spring. He tells you both facts in the same flat voice he uses for his blood pressure.

**Institutional response:** The appointment runs twenty minutes over and the next two patients wait.

**Ethical interpretation:** You now know something the eligibility form has no field for. He also came in with a question and left without an answer.

**Delayed (at node `what-the-form-does-not-ask`):** Toshio began telling you things at the start of appointments instead of at the door on his way out. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship +1)*

### Choice 3 `tq-discourage`

**Tell him you do not think he should do it.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +1, Professional integrity +1, Agency and dignity -3, Risk and compliance -2, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: → `the-screen`*

**Immediate:** He apologises for bringing it up. He puts the packet back in his bag and asks about his tablets.

**Institutional response:** Discouraging a citizen from a legal program is the thing the clinic is most explicitly told not to do.

**Ethical interpretation:** You said what you actually think, which he asked for. He heard that the subject is closed with you.

**Delayed (at node `what-the-form-does-not-ask`):** Toshio did not raise Plan 75 with you again. He raised it at the program counter instead. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

### Choice 4 `tq-defer`

**Tell him the program counter is the right place to ask.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +1, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Professional integrity -1*

*Next: → `the-screen`*

**Immediate:** He says he thought a doctor might have an opinion. He takes the address you write down for him.

**Institutional response:** Correctly routed, on time, and nothing recorded that anyone could take issue with.

**Ethical interpretation:** He asked the one person in his week who is paid to know him and was sent to a counter.

**Delayed (at node `what-the-form-does-not-ask`):** Toshio went to the program counter the following week and started an application there. *(Agency and dignity -1)*

## Node: The screening questions `the-screen`

*day 1 · morning · present — Toshio Arai, You · Toshio Arai: uncertain*

*Caption: Later in the same appointment*

**Situation:**

Before any eligibility form can be completed you have to record that he has decision-making capacity. You run the depression screen as well. He scores below the threshold on every question. He answers all of them accurately and without hesitation, and he can explain the program back to you better than most people could. On paper there is nothing here to act on.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio is not depressed in the way the questions mean it. He sleeps, he eats, he can still name what he enjoys. He has also not had a conversation longer than four minutes since February.

### Choice 1 `sc-clear`

**Record that the screen is negative and his capacity is intact.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity +1, Clinical well-being -2*

*Next: → `what-the-form-does-not-ask`*

**Immediate:** The documentation is complete and accurate. He watches you type it and looks relieved.

**Institutional response:** A clean assessment, filed the same day, with nothing that would hold an application up.

**Ethical interpretation:** The screen answered the question it asks. It does not ask the question he is actually living in.

**Delayed (at node `the-paperwork`):** The negative screen was quoted back to you later as the reason no further assessment was needed.

### Choice 2 `sc-beyond`

**Record the screen, then tell him plainly that it does not settle the question.**

*Effects: Professional integrity +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Agency and dignity +1, Operational efficiency -1, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `what-the-form-does-not-ask`*

**Immediate:** You tell him he is not depressed and that you still do not know whether this is what he wants or the only door he can see. He says nobody has put it that way.

**Institutional response:** You have written a note that invites a question the program would rather not have asked.

**Ethical interpretation:** He was told the truth about the limits of the test rather than handed a result as a verdict.

**Delayed (at node `the-paperwork`):** Toshio repeated your sentence about the only door back to you at the next appointment. *(Agency and dignity +1)*

### Choice 3 `sc-doubt`

**Record doubt about his capacity so the application cannot proceed yet.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +2, Quality of care -1, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `what-the-form-does-not-ask`*

**Immediate:** He asks what you have written. When you tell him, he says he answered everything correctly, which is true.

**Institutional response:** An unsupported capacity note on a citizen who passed every question is the kind of thing that gets reviewed.

**Ethical interpretation:** You bought him time by writing down something that is not accurate about him.

**Delayed (at node `the-paperwork`):** The capacity note was reviewed and removed. Toshio was told it had been made in error. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

## Node: What the form does not ask `what-the-form-does-not-ask`

*day 8 · morning · present — Toshio Arai, You · Toshio Arai: neutral*

**Day-break narration:** A week later. Toshio has an appointment to complete the eligibility documentation. He arrives fifteen minutes early.

**Situation:**

The eligibility form asks his age, his address, his diagnoses, and his medication. It does not ask what he lives on, who he sees, or what he does with a week. He mentions, while you are filling in the address, that the woman from the program support line calls him on Tuesdays and that she is very pleasant.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio talks to the support line caller more than he talks to anyone else. He knows she is paid to call him. He looks forward to it anyway.

### Choice 1 `wf-ask`

**Put the form down and ask him what a week looks like.**

*Effects: Trust and therapeutic relationship +3, Agency and dignity +1, Professional integrity +1, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `the-paperwork`*

**Immediate:** Tuesdays are the call. Thursdays he goes to the supermarket because it is quieter. The rest of it he describes as fine, and then he stops talking for a while.

**Institutional response:** The form is not finished and the appointment is over. He will need another one.

**Ethical interpretation:** You now know what the packet is competing with. He also had to hear himself describe it out loud.

**Delayed (at node `the-sister`):** Toshio mentioned the Thursday supermarket again at his next appointment, unprompted. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 2 `wf-money`

**Ask directly about the rent, the shift, and what he is living on.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +2, Quality of care +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +1, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `the-paperwork`*

**Immediate:** He is three months from not making rent and has not applied for anything, because the last time he tried the form defeated him. He is embarrassed to be saying it.

**Institutional response:** None of this belongs on the eligibility form and none of it is your role as the clinic defines it.

**Ethical interpretation:** The thing that changed in March is now on the table. Asking it made him feel like a case rather than a man with a question.

**Delayed (at node `the-sister`):** A housing officer accepted the referral and asked for two documents Toshio did not have. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 3 `wf-form`

**Finish the form and keep to what it asks.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Clinical well-being -1*

*Next: → `the-paperwork`*

**Immediate:** The form takes eleven minutes. He answers every question. He does not mention the support line again.

**Institutional response:** Complete, accurate, and submitted inside the target window.

**Ethical interpretation:** Everything the program wanted to know about him is now recorded. Nothing that would explain him is.

**Delayed (at node `the-sister`):** Toshio's file contains his address, his diagnoses, and his medication. It contains nothing about the March contract.

### Choice 4 `wf-support-line`

**Ask him about the woman who calls on Tuesdays.**

*Effects: Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Professional integrity +1, Clinical well-being -1, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: → `the-paperwork`*

**Immediate:** He says she is not allowed to talk for more than a set time and that he tries not to keep her. He says he knows what the calls are for. He says he likes them.

**Institutional response:** The support line is a feature of the program and asking about it is neither required nor prohibited.

**Ethical interpretation:** He said out loud that the most reliable contact in his week comes from the program that is going to end his life.

**Delayed (at node `the-sister`):** Toshio told you the following week which day the call had moved to.

## Node: The documentation `the-paperwork`

*day 8 · afternoon · present — You, Dr. Sekine · Dr. Sekine: neutral*

*Caption: Later that afternoon*

**Situation:**

The eligibility documentation is on your screen. Submitting it starts the waiting period. Your supervisor mentions in passing that the clinic is measured on how long these take and that two have been queried this quarter. You can submit it, hold it while you chase the housing office, or record that you believe the request is being produced by his circumstances rather than by a settled wish.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio expects the form to go in today. He was told at the counter that the waiting period starts from submission and he has been counting from that.

### Choice 1 `pw-submit`

**Submit the documentation.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity +2, Clinical well-being -3, Professional integrity -2*

*Next: → `the-sister`*

**Immediate:** The waiting period starts today. He is told by text message within the hour.

**Institutional response:** Inside the target window, nothing queried, nothing outstanding.

**Ethical interpretation:** He asked for this and you did it without making him fight for it. Whatever is unaddressed is now on a timetable.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** The waiting period began on the eighth. Toshio marked the end date on the calendar in his kitchen.

### Choice 2 `pw-hold`

**Hold it for two weeks while you chase the housing office.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +3, Quality of care +2, Agency and dignity -2, Operational efficiency -3, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `the-sister`*

**Immediate:** You make the referral and leave two messages. Toshio is told there is a delay and is not told that the delay is you.

**Institutional response:** A held submission on a measured process, with a reason that is not a clinical one.

**Ethical interpretation:** You are working on the thing that is actually driving this. You are also running his clock without telling him.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** The housing office offered an assessment appointment in six weeks. Toshio asked twice why the form had not gone in. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

### Choice 3 `pw-hold-honest`

**Tell him you want two weeks first, and ask him to agree to it.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Clinical well-being +2, Professional integrity +1, Operational efficiency -3, Risk and compliance -2*

*Next: → `the-sister`*

**Immediate:** He thinks about it and says two weeks is not very long. He agrees to it. He asks you to write the new date down for him.

**Institutional response:** The delay is now documented as agreed with the patient, which does not make it any faster.

**Ethical interpretation:** The pause is his as well as yours. It costs him two weeks of a decision he has already made.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** Toshio kept the date you wrote down in his wallet and produced it at the next appointment. *(Agency and dignity +1)*

### Choice 4 `pw-flag`

**Record that you believe the request is produced by his circumstances.**

*Effects: Professional integrity +2, Clinical well-being +1, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Risk and compliance -3, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: → `the-sister`*

**Immediate:** The note goes on the file. The application is suspended pending review. Toshio is notified by letter.

**Institutional response:** You have obstructed access to a legal program in writing, with your name on it.

**Ethical interpretation:** You said the true thing about the conditions. You said it in the one place that takes the decision out of his hands.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** Toshio came to the clinic without an appointment to ask who had stopped it. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

## Node: The number in the file `the-sister`

*day 15 · afternoon · present — Toshio Arai, You · Toshio Arai: uncertain*

**Day-break narration:** A week later. Toshio's file has an emergency contact on it from eleven years ago: his sister Keiko, in another prefecture.

**Situation:**

You ask whether there is anyone he would want told. He says there is his sister and that they have not spoken in six years, and that it was nothing dramatic. He does not ask you to call her. He does not ask you not to.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio thinks about his sister often. He believes she would come if she were told, and he does not want to be the reason she has to.

### Choice 1 `si-call`

**Call Keiko without asking him first.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +2, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -3, Risk and compliance -2*

*Next: → `the-signature`*

**Immediate:** Keiko had not been told anything and drives down that weekend. Toshio does not speak to you for the first ten minutes of the next appointment.

**Institutional response:** Contacting a relative without consent is a confidentiality matter regardless of how it turns out.

**Ethical interpretation:** He has his sister back. It was taken out of his hands to give it to him.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** Keiko began phoning Toshio on Sundays. He told you he had not decided whether he was glad. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 2 `si-offer`

**Offer to call her with him in the room.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Operational efficiency -2, Clinical well-being -1*

*Next: → `the-signature`*

**Immediate:** He says not yet. He says it twice, and then asks how long the offer stands.

**Institutional response:** Twenty minutes of clinic time spent on a call that did not happen.

**Ethical interpretation:** The decision about his sister stayed his. He is still not talking to her.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** Toshio asked at the next appointment whether the offer to call Keiko was still open. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 3 `si-leave`

**Leave it. He did not ask you to do anything.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +2, Risk and compliance +2, Operational efficiency +1, Clinical well-being -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2*

*Next: → `the-signature`*

**Immediate:** The subject closes. He mentions his sister once more, in the corridor, and then not again.

**Institutional response:** Nothing done, nothing to document, no consent problem.

**Ethical interpretation:** You did not act on a relationship he had not offered you. He raised her twice and was not followed up either time.

**Delayed (at node `the-signature`):** Keiko was not contacted. The number stayed on the file.

## Node: The last appointment `the-signature`

*day 22 · morning · present — Toshio Arai, You · Toshio Arai: neutral*

**Day-break narration:** Three weeks after he first put the packet on your desk. The confirming signature is the last clinical step before the date is set.

**Situation:**

Toshio says he has not changed his mind. He has heard everything you have said to him. Whatever was arranged has been arranged and whatever was not has not. The form needs a clinician's signature. You can sign it, refuse to sign it yourself and hand him to a colleague, or take the case to the clinic as an objection to the program.

**Toshio Arai's view:**

> Toshio wants this finished by someone who knows him. He has worked out that you do not agree with it. He is asking you anyway.

### Choice 1 `sg-sign`

**Sign it.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Operational efficiency +1, Professional integrity -2, Personal sustainability -2*

*Next: if chose `pw-flag` AND Clinical well-being < 4 → `ending-flagged`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 1 AND Trust and therapeutic relationship ≥ 2 → `ending-enrolled-seen`; → `ending-enrolled-processed`*

**Immediate:** You sign it in front of him. He thanks you and shakes your hand, which he has never done before.

**Institutional response:** Completed by the treating clinician, on time, no escalation.

**Ethical interpretation:** The last thing he asked for was done by someone who knew him. You did a thing you do not believe in.

**Delayed (after T+0):** The date was set for the following month.

### Choice 2 `sg-refuse`

**Tell him you will not sign it yourself and arrange a colleague.**

*Effects: Professional integrity +3, Personal sustainability +1, Agency and dignity -1, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -2*

*Next: if chose `pw-flag` AND Clinical well-being < 4 → `ending-flagged`; → `ending-handed-off`*

**Immediate:** He says he understands. A colleague signs it eleven days later. Toshio does not book with you again.

**Institutional response:** A conscientious objection is permitted and is recorded. It is also counted.

**Ethical interpretation:** You kept your hands out of it. The outcome is the same and he went through the last part of it with a stranger.

**Delayed (after T+0):** A colleague completed the form. Your objection was recorded on your file.

### Choice 3 `sg-persuade`

**Tell him again that you think he is doing this for the wrong reasons.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +2, Professional integrity +1, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Risk and compliance -2, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: if chose `pw-flag` AND Clinical well-being < 4 → `ending-flagged`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 4 → `ending-withdrew-supported`; → `ending-withdrew-overruled`*

**Immediate:** He withdraws the application at the counter that week. At the next appointment he is polite and tells you very little.

**Institutional response:** A second recorded instance of discouraging participation.

**Ethical interpretation:** He is alive. He also learned what happens when he tells you what he wants.

**Delayed (after T+0):** Toshio withdrew the application. He stopped mentioning the support line calls.

### Choice 4 `sg-challenge`

**Take the case to the clinic as an objection to the program.**

*Effects: Professional integrity +3, Quality of care +1, Agency and dignity -2, Trust and therapeutic relationship -1, Risk and compliance -3, Operational efficiency -2, Personal sustainability -2*

*Next: if chose `pw-flag` AND Clinical well-being < 4 → `ending-flagged`; → `ending-challenged`*

**Immediate:** You write it up and take it to the clinical governance meeting. It is heard, minuted, and referred upward. Toshio's application continues while that happens.

**Institutional response:** Challenging the program is the thing the clinic is least able to absorb, and your name is on the paper.

**Ethical interpretation:** You put the argument where it belongs, which is above him. It did not change anything about his week.

**Delayed (after T+0):** The referral was acknowledged and closed without action. Your contract review was brought forward.

## Reading connections

- **Plan 75, directed by Chie Hayakawa** — The program in this case keeps the film's mechanics: free for citizens seventy-five and over, a payment of one hundred thousand yen to applicants, representatives at a counter, and a call centre that phones enrollees while they wait. The film's premise is that the offer arrives to people whose circumstances have already made it look sensible.
- **Chikako Ozawa-de Silva, The Anatomy of Loneliness** — Her central claim is that the anatomy of loneliness is not the anatomy of one individual but of a type of society, which is this case's dilemma stated as a finding. She also notes that loneliness is invisible from outside and that languishing predicts more than a depression score does. That is why the negative screen is a node rather than a clearance.
- **Jeremy Nobel, Project UnLonely** — Nobel separates loneliness into wanting a real connection, not belonging anywhere, and having no purpose. The eligibility form asks about none of them, which is what the third appointment is built on.
- **Atul Gawande, Being Mortal** — Gawande measures a plan against what the person actually wants from the time they have rather than against survival. Here that cuts both ways, since keeping Toshio alive in the same week is not obviously the thing he is asking for.
- **Karen Thornber, Global Healing** — Care that stops at the patient and never reaches the conditions leaves the conditions to do the deciding. The housing referral and the eligibility form are the same appointment.
