# The unexplained illness

> **For reviewers.** This is the complete text of one case, in reading
> order. You do not need to use the app. Questions worth answering as you read:
>
> 1. **Does any choice read as obviously correct?** Every option should be
>    defensible from some stakeholder's position. Flag any that isn't.
> 2. **Are the patients people, or teaching devices?** Flag anything that
>    reduces a character to their diagnosis, identity, or a lesson.
> 3. **Is the clinical content accurate and current?**
> 4. **Does the feedback explain rather than grade?** Flag anything that
>    reads as "right answer / wrong answer."
> 5. **Whose experience is missing or misrepresented?**
>
> Send notes back in any form. Review status only changes to
> `expert-reviewed` after a named reviewer with relevant lived or
> professional expertise has actually read the case.

- **Case id:** `the-unexplained-illness` (v1)
- **Setting:** General practice, the eleventh visit in a year
- **Difficulty:** advanced · **Modes:** deliberative · **Scoring:** standard
- **Review status:** draft

## Characters

### You (clinician)

### Priya Raman (patient)

> Priya Raman: thirty-three, a warehouse team leader. For a year she has had fatigue, gut symptoms, pain in no particular place, fog, dizziness, and neurological complaints that come and go. Bloodwork, imaging, and four specialists have found nothing. She has started losing shifts.

### Ms. Adeyemi (supervisor)

## Learning objectives

- Care for someone whose suffering is real and whose illness is not measurable
- Say that you do not know without withdrawing from the patient
- Offer mental health support as part of care rather than as the exit
- Write a chart entry that protects the patient from the next clinician

## Node: The eleventh visit `the-eleventh-visit` *(start)*

*day 1 · morning · present — Priya Raman, You · Priya Raman: exhausted*

**Situation:**

Priya Raman is here for the eleventh time in a year. She is worse than she was in the spring and she has started losing shifts. Everything has come back normal again. Before you went in, the clinic administrator flagged her chart as excessive resource use and told you that further low yield testing will show on your evaluation.

**Priya Raman's view:**

> Priya has been told eleven times that her results are normal. She has stopped expecting an answer and has not stopped needing one. She is here because she is worse and because there is nowhere else to go.

### Choice 1 `ev-test`

**Order the next round of tests.**

*Effects: Quality of care +1, Trust and therapeutic relationship +1, Operational efficiency -3, Risk and compliance -2, Clinical well-being -1*

*Next: → `the-folder`*

**Immediate:** She agrees to all of it. Two of the tests need a day off work she does not have, and one of them she pays for herself.

**Institutional response:** Four more investigations on a chart that is already flagged.

**Ethical interpretation:** You are still looking, which is what she came for. She is paying for the looking in shifts and in money.

**Delayed (at node `the-word`):** Three of the four results were normal. The fourth was marginal and was repeated twice.

### Choice 2 `ev-pause`

**Tell her you are pausing further testing for now.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity -2, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2*

*Next: → `the-folder`*

**Immediate:** She asks what she is supposed to do in the meantime. You do not have an answer prepared for that.

**Institutional response:** The flag comes off the chart within the week.

**Ethical interpretation:** You stopped exposing her to tests that were not helping. You stopped without putting anything in their place.

**Delayed (at node `the-word`):** Priya cancelled one appointment and rebooked it twice. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

### Choice 3 `ev-name`

**Tell her plainly that you do not know what this is.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Professional integrity +2, Operational efficiency -1, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `the-folder`*

**Immediate:** She says that is the first time anyone has said it. She asks whether that means she is being dropped, and you tell her it does not.

**Institutional response:** Nothing in the flag changes. Nothing you said is billable.

**Ethical interpretation:** You told her the truth about the limits of what you can see. She still leaves without treatment.

**Delayed (at node `the-word`):** Priya told the receptionist she wanted her next appointment with you rather than whoever was free. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship +1)*

### Choice 4 `ev-refer`

**Refer her to a fifth specialist.**

*Effects: Quality of care +1, Trust and therapeutic relationship +1, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -1, Professional integrity -1*

*Next: → `the-folder`*

**Immediate:** The wait is nineteen weeks. She takes the letter and thanks you for it.

**Institutional response:** A fifth referral on a flagged chart is the specific thing the administrator raised with you.

**Ethical interpretation:** Somebody else will look at her with fresh eyes. For nineteen weeks she has a letter instead of a plan.

**Delayed (at node `the-word`):** The fifth specialist wrote back that the presentation was outside their area and suggested a sixth. *(Agency and dignity -1)*

## Node: The folder `the-folder`

*day 1 · morning · present — Priya Raman, You · Priya Raman: uncertain*

*Caption: Later in the same appointment*

**Situation:**

Priya takes out a folder. It has printed studies, a list of conditions she wants ruled out, and a symptom diary going back fourteen months. Some of what she has printed is wrong. Two of the papers are better than the last specialist letter in her file. The diary shows her symptoms clustering after her night rotations.

**Priya Raman's view:**

> Priya expects to be told she should stay off the internet. She has been told that twice. She kept the diary because nobody else was keeping one.

### Choice 1 `fo-read`

**Go through the diary with her and use what is in it.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Quality of care +2, Operational efficiency -3*

*Next: → `the-word`*

**Immediate:** The clustering after night rotations is real and neither of you had seen it laid out before. The appointment runs twenty five minutes over.

**Institutional response:** The clinic runs late for the rest of the morning.

**Ethical interpretation:** Her fourteen months of work became clinical information. It took the time of two other appointments to do it.

**Delayed (at node `what-she-lives-above`):** Priya brought the diary up to date before every appointment after that. *(Quality of care +1)*

### Choice 2 `fo-dismiss`

**Tell her that searching online is making this harder.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +1, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -3*

*Next: → `the-word`*

**Immediate:** She puts the folder back in her bag. She does not take it out again at any later appointment.

**Institutional response:** The appointment finishes on time and no new lines of investigation are opened.

**Ethical interpretation:** You closed the one piece of work she was able to do about her own illness.

**Delayed (at node `what-she-lives-above`):** Priya stopped bringing the diary. She kept writing it. *(Agency and dignity -1)*

### Choice 3 `fo-split`

**Take the diary and set the printed studies aside.**

*Effects: Quality of care +1, Operational efficiency +1, Agency and dignity -1, Trust and therapeutic relationship -1*

*Next: → `the-word`*

**Immediate:** She notices which pile went where. She does not say anything about it.

**Institutional response:** A workable compromise that costs the clinic almost nothing.

**Ethical interpretation:** You took the part that was useful to you. She had sorted both piles herself and knows which one you did not want.

**Delayed (at node `what-she-lives-above`):** Priya brought the diary to later appointments and left the papers at home.

## Node: What you call it `the-word`

*day 30 · morning · present — Priya Raman, You · Priya Raman: uncertain*

**Day-break narration:** A month later. Priya has dropped to part time. She asks what you are going to write on the form for her employer.

**Situation:**

Her employer wants something in writing and so does she. Whatever you call this will follow her. She has already been told once by a specialist that there is a large stress component, and she has looked up what that means when it is written down.

**Priya Raman's view:**

> Priya is not against seeing someone about how she is coping. She is against it being the end of the conversation, because the last time it was offered, everything else stopped.

### Choice 1 `wd-uncertain`

**Say the symptoms are real, the cause is not established, and both are true at once.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +3, Professional integrity +2, Operational efficiency -1, Risk and compliance -2*

*Next: → `what-she-lives-above`*

**Immediate:** She asks you to write it in exactly those words. You do.

**Institutional response:** An open ended formulation on a flagged chart keeps the case open indefinitely.

**Ethical interpretation:** She has something in writing that does not require her to be either diagnosed or doubted.

**Delayed (at node `the-chart`):** Her employer accepted the letter and moved her off night rotations for three months. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 2 `wd-psych-part`

**Offer a mental health referral as one part of the plan and say that it is one part.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +2, Quality of care +2, Operational efficiency -1, Agency and dignity -1*

*Next: → `what-she-lives-above`*

**Immediate:** She accepts it once you say out loud that the rest of the workup is not being closed.

**Institutional response:** A referral into a service with a shorter wait than any of the specialists.

**Ethical interpretation:** She gets help with the year she has had. The offer still lands on a woman who has been told this before and meant differently.

**Delayed (at node `the-chart`):** Priya attended six sessions. She said they helped with sleeping and did nothing for the dizziness. *(Clinical well-being +1)*

### Choice 3 `wd-psych-exit`

**Tell her the tests are normal and this is most likely stress.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -3, Clinical well-being -2*

*Next: → `what-she-lives-above`*

**Immediate:** She says all right. She does not argue and she does not ask anything else for the rest of the appointment.

**Institutional response:** The investigation closes and the flag comes off. This is the outcome the clinic was asking for.

**Ethical interpretation:** You gave the explanation that requires nothing further from anyone. She has been unwell for a year and is now unwell and not believed.

**Delayed (at node `the-chart`):** Priya did not book a follow up. The next contact on her record is a repeat prescription request. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

### Choice 4 `wd-physio`

**Commit to a physical cause you cannot name yet.**

*Effects: Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Agency and dignity +1, Professional integrity -1, Risk and compliance -2, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: → `what-she-lives-above`*

**Immediate:** She is relieved. She asks what it might be and you have to tell her you were not saying you knew.

**Institutional response:** Committing in writing to a cause you have not established is the kind of note that gets read back to you later.

**Ethical interpretation:** You put yourself on her side of it. You did that by promising something you cannot deliver.

**Delayed (at node `the-chart`):** Priya asked at the next appointment which physical cause you had meant. *(Trust and therapeutic relationship -1)*

## Node: Where she spends her time `what-she-lives-above`

*day 30 · morning · present — Priya Raman, You · Priya Raman: neutral*

*Caption: Later in the same appointment*

**Situation:**

In fourteen months of notes, four specialist letters, and eleven appointments, nobody has written down what she does for nine hours a day or what is under her flat. The diary puts her worst weeks after night rotations in the older part of the warehouse.

**Priya Raman's view:**

> Priya has thought about the warehouse. She has not raised it because she assumed it was not the kind of thing a doctor asks about, and because she needs the job.

### Choice 1 `ex-ask`

**Ask what she works around and what she lives above.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +2, Quality of care +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +1, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -1*

*Next: → `the-chart`*

**Immediate:** Diesel forklifts run indoors on the night rotation because the electric ones are on day shift. Her flat is above a unit that was a dry cleaner until two years ago. Neither fact is in her record anywhere.

**Institutional response:** None of this maps onto a test the clinic can order or bill.

**Ethical interpretation:** You asked the question nobody had asked. You cannot yet tell her whether any of it matters.

**Delayed (at node `the-flag`):** Priya asked her employer for the ventilation records. She was told they would look for them. *(Agency and dignity +1)*

### Choice 2 `ex-occ`

**Refer her to occupational health.**

*Effects: Quality of care +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +1, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -2, Clinical well-being -1*

*Next: → `the-chart`*

**Immediate:** The referral goes through her employer, which she did not expect and does not like. She asks whether it can be anonymous.

**Institutional response:** An external referral on a flagged chart, and one that puts the clinic between an employee and her employer.

**Ethical interpretation:** The exposure question is now being asked by someone with the authority to measure it. It is being asked in front of the people who pay her.

**Delayed (at node `the-flag`):** Occupational health visited the site and recorded that ventilation was within limits on the day they attended.

### Choice 3 `ex-skip`

**Keep to the clinical history.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +1, Agency and dignity -2, Quality of care -2*

*Next: → `the-chart`*

**Immediate:** The appointment covers the same ground as the previous ten. She does not raise the warehouse.

**Institutional response:** Nothing opened, nothing added, appointment finished on time.

**Ethical interpretation:** The history is now complete by the standard the clinic uses. Nine hours of her day are still not in it.

**Delayed (at node `the-flag`):** The warehouse does not appear anywhere in Priya's record.

## Node: What goes in the chart `the-chart`

*day 90 · afternoon · present — You · *

**Day-break narration:** Two months later. Whatever you write now is what the next clinician reads before they meet her.

**Situation:**

You are writing the summary that will sit at the top of her record. She will move practice eventually, or you will be away, and this paragraph is what the next person sees first. It decides whether they start from her symptoms or from her file.

### Choice 1 `ch-credible`

**Write that the illness is unexplained and credible.**

*Effects: Agency and dignity +3, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Professional integrity +2, Risk and compliance -3, Operational efficiency -1*

*Next: → `the-flag`*

**Immediate:** The entry says the symptoms are consistent across fourteen months, that no cause has been established, and that the reporting has been reliable throughout.

**Institutional response:** An open case with no diagnosis and no closure date, in writing, under your name.

**Ethical interpretation:** The next clinician meets a credible patient with an unsolved problem. Nobody is ever going to be able to close this file.

**Delayed (after T+0):** A locum read the summary and ran the new symptom rather than the file.

### Choice 2 `ch-neutral`

**Write that no organic cause has been identified.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +2, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -1*

*Next: → `the-flag`*

**Immediate:** The entry is accurate and complete. It contains no judgment of any kind about her.

**Institutional response:** Defensible, neutral, and exactly the phrasing the clinic prefers.

**Ethical interpretation:** Every word is true. Read cold by a stranger in four minutes, it says she has been looked at and nothing was found.

**Delayed (after T+0):** A locum read the summary and told Priya her workup had been thorough. *(Agency and dignity -1)*

### Choice 3 `ch-somatic`

**Write that the presentation is consistent with a somatic symptom disorder.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +3, Agency and dignity -3, Trust and therapeutic relationship -2, Clinical well-being -2*

*Next: → `the-flag`*

**Immediate:** The entry gives the record a diagnosis it can use. Nothing further is expected of anyone.

**Institutional response:** A named condition closes the case and takes the chart off the flagged list permanently.

**Ethical interpretation:** You wrote the one sentence that will be read before her symptoms are, for the rest of her care.

**Delayed (after T+0):** Priya's next two presentations were recorded under the same heading without further examination. *(Clinical well-being -1)*

## Node: The flagged chart `the-flag`

*day 90 · afternoon · present — You, Ms. Adeyemi · Ms. Adeyemi: neutral*

*Caption: Later that afternoon*

**Situation:**

The clinic administrator has Priya's chart on the screen. Eleven appointments, five referrals, and no diagnosis. She asks what your plan is for closing it. The measure counts appointments and tests. It does not have a field for a patient who is still unwell.

**Priya Raman's view:**

> Priya does not know the chart is flagged. She has been booking the earliest appointment she can get for a year because the later ones cost her a shift.

### Choice 1 `fl-plan`

**Write a plan with her: what to watch for, when to come back, what you will not repeat.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +3, Agency and dignity +2, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Quality of care +2, Operational efficiency -2, Risk and compliance -2, Personal sustainability -1*

*Next: if Trust and therapeutic relationship < -3 → `ending-quiet-discharge`; if chose `wd-psych-exit` OR chose `ch-somatic` AND Agency and dignity < 0 → `ending-psychologised`; if chose `fl-challenge` → `ending-you-flagged`; if chose `ev-test` AND Operational efficiency < -4 → `ending-diagnosis-late`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 2 AND Trust and therapeutic relationship ≥ 3 → `ending-plan-holds`; → `ending-loop`*

**Immediate:** The plan fits on one page. It lists what would change your mind, the two things worth treating now, and the tests that are not going to be repeated.

**Institutional response:** The appointments continue, at a lower rate, with no end date.

**Ethical interpretation:** She has something to do and a reason to come back that is not another normal result.

**Delayed (after T+0):** Priya used the plan twice in the following six months. She did not attend as an emergency again.

### Choice 2 `fl-absorb`

**Keep seeing her and say nothing about the flag.**

*Effects: Clinical well-being +1, Trust and therapeutic relationship +2, Agency and dignity +1, Operational efficiency -2, Personal sustainability -2*

*Next: if Trust and therapeutic relationship < -3 → `ending-quiet-discharge`; if chose `wd-psych-exit` OR chose `ch-somatic` AND Agency and dignity < 0 → `ending-psychologised`; if chose `fl-challenge` → `ending-you-flagged`; if chose `ev-test` AND Operational efficiency < -4 → `ending-diagnosis-late`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 2 AND Trust and therapeutic relationship ≥ 3 → `ending-plan-holds`; → `ending-loop`*

**Immediate:** You keep booking her in. Your numbers carry it and nobody raises it with you again this quarter.

**Institutional response:** The flag stays on the chart and now sits against your name too.

**Ethical interpretation:** She keeps her doctor. The cost of that is being paid quietly by you and will be paid again next quarter.

**Delayed (after T+0):** Your appointment numbers were raised at your review. Priya was not mentioned by name.

### Choice 3 `fl-comply`

**Close the investigation and space her appointments out.**

*Effects: Operational efficiency +3, Risk and compliance +2, Agency and dignity -2, Trust and therapeutic relationship -3, Clinical well-being -2*

*Next: if Trust and therapeutic relationship < -3 → `ending-quiet-discharge`; if chose `wd-psych-exit` OR chose `ch-somatic` AND Agency and dignity < 0 → `ending-psychologised`; if chose `fl-challenge` → `ending-you-flagged`; if chose `ev-test` AND Operational efficiency < -4 → `ending-diagnosis-late`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 2 AND Trust and therapeutic relationship ≥ 3 → `ending-plan-holds`; → `ending-loop`*

**Immediate:** Her next available appointment is in eleven weeks. She takes it and asks what she should do before then.

**Institutional response:** The chart comes off the flagged list and your numbers recover inside a month.

**Ethical interpretation:** The resource problem is solved. She is exactly as unwell as she was and now waits three months to say so.

**Delayed (after T+0):** Priya did not attend the eleven week appointment.

### Choice 4 `fl-challenge`

**Tell her the measure is wrong and put that in writing.**

*Effects: Professional integrity +3, Quality of care +1, Risk and compliance -3, Operational efficiency -2, Personal sustainability -2*

*Next: if Trust and therapeutic relationship < -3 → `ending-quiet-discharge`; if chose `wd-psych-exit` OR chose `ch-somatic` AND Agency and dignity < 0 → `ending-psychologised`; if chose `fl-challenge` → `ending-you-flagged`; if chose `ev-test` AND Operational efficiency < -4 → `ending-diagnosis-late`; if Clinical well-being ≥ 2 AND Trust and therapeutic relationship ≥ 3 → `ending-plan-holds`; → `ending-loop`*

**Immediate:** You write that a measure counting appointments and tests cannot represent a patient who is undiagnosed and still unwell. It is acknowledged and forwarded.

**Institutional response:** You have disputed the metric you are assessed on, in writing, with your name on it.

**Ethical interpretation:** You put the argument where the problem actually is. It changes nothing about her week.

**Delayed (after T+0):** The submission was acknowledged. The measure was not changed and your review was brought forward.

## Reading connections

- **Meghan O'Rourke, The Invisible Kingdom** — O'Rourke describes doctors who dismissed her once the labs came back normal, and the tendency to disbelieve sick people who lack a diagnosis and insist they are ill. She names the diagnostic gaze as limited by bias, institutional pressure, and epistemic hierarchy. This case is built so that the disbelief, rather than the uncertainty, is what does the damage.
- **Scott Slovic, Swarnalatha Rangarajan and Vidya Sarveswaran, Toward a Medical-Environmental Humanities** — Their volume works from transcorporeality, the porousness of bodies to what is around them, and from slow violence and precarity, harm that is gradual and dispersed and so is never counted as harm. That is why the warehouse and the unit under her flat are a clinical question, and why nobody had written either of them down.
- **Alan Bleakley, The Medical Humanities Come of Age** — Interpretation is part of the work rather than a supplement to it. The symptom diary is fourteen months of data that only becomes clinical information if somebody reads it as such.
- **Karen Thornber, Global Healing** — Care that reaches only as far as the test results leaves the conditions untouched. The chart entry and the flag on it are part of her treatment.
